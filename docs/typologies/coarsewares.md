# Coarse & cooking wares

Scope: utilitarian kitchen and storage vessels — cooking pots, casseroles, lids, basins/mortaria, jugs, table-amphorae, pithoi/dolia. Load this file when the triage key in `CLAUDE.md` points to "coarse & cooking ware."

## Diagnostic key

- **Use traces:** external sooting, burnt residue, or oxidised/reduced patches → cooking vessel. Mortaria show interior grit and wear.
- **Fabric:** coarse, with deliberate temper (sand, grog, calcite, volcanic) chosen for thermal shock resistance; African cooking ware has a polished "black-top" rim band.
- **Form/part:** rim profile is primary — everted cooking-pot rims, flanged casserole/lid-seated rims, hooked mortarium rims, thickened pithos rims. Handles and bases secondary.
- **Surface:** burnish, combing, wavy-line incision, or a painted band; African cooking ware burnished red-to-black surface.
- **Region over series:** coarse wares are strongly regional and often published site-by-site, so name the regional series and period rather than forcing a Mediterranean-wide type.

## Named frameworks

- **African cooking ware:** Hayes forms (cooking-ware series in *Late Roman Pottery*); Atlante I; the classic casseroles/lids/dishes with black-top burnish.
- **Pompeian Red Ware** (internal red-slipped baking dishes/lids), early Imperial west.
- **Aegean / Eastern cooking wares:** regional Late Roman cooking pots; Agora (Athenian Agora volumes) for Athens; chytra/lopas/caccabus forms.
- **Punic & Iberian coarse wares:** regional Punic kitchen series (handmade and wheelmade); Iberian common wares.
- **Byzantine:** glazed and unglazed cooking/common wares published in regional corpora (e.g. Saraçhane for Constantinople).
- **Storage:** dolia / pithoi (regional); often described rather than type-numbered.
