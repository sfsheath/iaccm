# Fine wares — tableware

Scope: slipped and gloss tablewares — cups, bowls, dishes, plates. Load this file when the triage key in `CLAUDE.md` points to "fine ware / tableware."

## Diagnostic key

- **Surface:** colour and quality of slip/gloss is primary.
  - Glossy black → Black Gloss / Campana (Hellenistic–Republican).
  - Glossy/satin red, fine fabric → terra sigillata (Italian or Gaulish) or Eastern Sigillata.
  - Matte-to-semi-lustrous red slip, often with a darker rim band or "stacking" scorch → African Red Slip (ARS).
  - Red slip with a distinctive rim, micaceous fabric, eastern Aegean → Phocaean Red Slip (Late Roman C).
- **Form/part:** rim profile and foot-ring are the strongest shape clues; flanged bowls, rouletted rims, and floor decoration matter.
- **Decoration:** rouletting, appliqué, stamps (potter's name-stamps on Italian sigillata; stamped crosses/animals on ARS floors), relief moulding (Arretine relief, Gaulish Dragendorff 37).
- **Fabric:** fine, well-levigated; ARS is a brick-red Tunisian fabric; ESB has a characteristic fabric/slip; Phocaean is micaceous.

## Named frameworks

- **Black Gloss:** Campana A / B / C (Morel typology, *Céramique campanienne*).
- **Italian Sigillata:** Arretine; *Conspectus* form numbers (Conspectus formarum terrae sigillatae italico modo confectae); Dragendorff numbers also used.
- **Gaulish Sigillata:** South Gaulish (La Graufesenque), Central Gaulish (Lezoux), East Gaulish; Dragendorff (Drag. 18, 27, 29, 37…), Curle, Déchelette forms.
- **African Red Slip:** **Hayes** form numbers (Hayes 1972, *Late Roman Pottery*); the central reference for ARS. Atlante delle forme ceramiche I also used.
- **Phocaean Red Slip / Late Roman C:** Hayes forms (Hayes 1, 3, 10…).
- **Eastern Sigillata:** Eastern Sigillata A (ESA), B (ESB), C/D; Atlante II / Hayes forms.

## Corrections & field notes

- **2026-06-13 — Red-slip "dish base" was a lid.** A brick-red, matte-to-semi-lustrous slipped fragment with a central projecting feature and concentric grooves + a rouletted band was first read as the floor + low ring-foot of an open ARS dish. **User correction: it is a lid.** The "ring-foot" is the lid's central **knob/handle**; the grooved/rouletted surface is the **top of the lid**, not a vessel floor.
  - *Lesson:* on a slipped open fragment, a single central projection with decoration radiating around it is as likely a lid knob (held from above) as a foot-ring (stands below). Check the profile for which way the wall flares and where any sooting/wear sits before assuming "base." Concentric rouletting centered on the knob favors a lid top.
  - *Final ID (user-confirmed):* **DICOCER CLAIR-C Dn9.11** — *couvercle conique à ergot interne et bord relevé* (conical ARS-C lid with internal seating lug and an everted/raised rim), = Deneauve 1974 fig. 9 no. 11, = Atlante I fig. XXXI, 10. Chrono. 450/520. See `SOURCES.md` → CLAIR-C, printed p. 188.
  - *Diagnostic that decided it:* the **everted/raised rim** (*bord relevé*) + internal lug, vs. the plain rounded edge of CLAIR-D At53.5 and the projecting collar of CLAIR-A 16. Knob + rouletted slope is shared across all three African lid forms and does **not** discriminate.
  - *Process failure to design against:* the candidate was pruned because a text-layer search for "couvercle" returned a blank (the wrapped description broke the regex) and CLAIR-C was never visually inspected. **Never prune a ware/category on a lossy text signal; render and look.** This case is the first regression test in `eval/`.
